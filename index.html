Poisson arrivals and the finite queue

Submissions arrive at rate λ per minute. Independent gaps have density f(t) = λ · exp(−λt) and expected length 1/λ minutes. Each of c volunteers completes reviews at exponential rate μ per minute. Waiting requests enter service in arrival order. K counts both waiting and active submissions.

Set w₀ = 1 and compute wₙ = wₙ₋₁ · λ / [min(n, c) · μ]. Normalize by the sum of weights to get Pₙ. A submission is blocked with probability Pₖ. Admitted throughput λₑ = λ · (1 − Pₖ). Average queue length Lq = Σ max(0, n − c) · Pₙ, so mean wait Wq = Lq / λₑ. Wait excludes service time.

The snapshots start with an empty desk. Headline queue metrics describe steady state, including overload because capacity is finite.

Absorbing screening chain

For synthetic suspicious submissions, New stays New with probability 0.20 or enters Review with probability 0.80. Review stays in Review with probability 0.25. On exit, a fraction q is Held and the rest is Missed. Both outcomes are absorbing. Each step updates the row vector pₜ₊₁ = pₜ · P, starting from (1, 0, 0, 0).

The expected steps to absorption are 1/0.80 + 1/0.75 ≈ 2.58. Terminal held probability is q. Steps are screening decisions with no fixed duration.

FAIR-inspired loss model

Generate 10,000 synthetic years. Potential incidents have Poisson frequency f per year. Independent screening thins that count to Poisson(f · (1 − q)). Each missed incident costs ₱4,000 · exp(0.6Z), with Z standard normal. Costs are hypothetical response and recovery expenses.

Annual loss expectancy is the mean of simulated yearly totals. The 95th percentile is the nearest-rank value. The analytic mean is f · (1 − q) · ₱4,000 · exp(0.6²/2). This simplified model uses fixed input frequency and independent losses. It represents neither assessed costs nor legal fines.

Reference entropy and rate limiting

A synthetic reference has L independent uniform characters drawn from an alphabet of size A. That gives S = Aᴸ possible values and Shannon entropy H = log₂(S) bits. Under uniformly ordered, distinct checks, the average position is (S + 1)/2. A cap of r checks per second gives expected time (S + 1)/(2r). The Lab models an idealized reference policy using aggregate mathematics.

Mixed strategy for random review

A submitter chooses genuine or misuse. Misuse earns G points if skipped and incurs penalty F if reviewed. Reviewing costs C points. A successful interception credits the desk F points and a missed misuse costs the desk G. Indifference gives review probability p = G/(G + F) and abuse probability q = C/(G + F). The Lab fixes G at 300 points and keeps 0 < C < G + F. Payoffs are hypothetical.

Sliding-window submission anomalies

Use the previous six one-minute counts to compute population mean μ and standard deviation σ. Flag a current count x when Z = |x − μ|/σ ≥ τ. With zero variance, an unchanged count scores zero and any change is flagged. The dashed lines show the current window’s thresholds, with the lower bound clipped to zero for display. A flag prompts review and can also reflect a legitimate book-drive surge.

Series and parallel availability

MTBF and MTTR are hours. Inherent component availability is MTBF/(MTBF + MTTR). The shared upstream link is in series with hosting, so multiply their availabilities. Independent identical hosts operating in parallel give hosting availability 1 − (1 − A)² under immediate failover. Expected unavailable hours per year are 8,760 · (1 − Aₛᵧₛ). A shared upstream outage affects both hosts.

Privacy and defensive controls

For a real donation or inquiry workflow, RA 10173 Section 11 informs limiting data collection and retention to the stated purpose. Section 20 informs proportionate safeguards for submission records, including staff access restrictions and incident handling. Here, screening is represented by bounded scenario parameters and the simulator processes synthetic data.
